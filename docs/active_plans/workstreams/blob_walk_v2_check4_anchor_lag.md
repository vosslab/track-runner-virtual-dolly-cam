## Anchor-age derivation

The acceptance box at the time each frame is **observed** is centered on
`last_accepted`, which updates only when an EMITTED (oldest-window) frame is accepted
(audit P8, `walk_walker.py:1036-1039, 377-379`). The window depth is 9 frames.

For a row emitted at frame N with `window_head_frame = W`:

```
anchor_age_at_observation = |W - anchor_frame_at_observation|
```

where `anchor_frame_at_observation` = the last accepted frame emitted BEFORE frame N was
processed. In steady state (every frame accepted):

```
anchor_age_at_observation = W - (N - 1) = (N + 8) - (N - 1) = 9
```

The `window_head_frame` column (added in P15 / schema v14) makes this derivation direct
and unambiguous. Bootstrap rows (step=0) have anchor_age=0 by definition.

For stall passes (anchor frozen at seed S), anchor_age_at_observation grows unboundedly:
each successive window head frame W = S + 9, S + 10, ... raises the age linearly.

## Two distinct stall mechanisms

The two diagnosed stall cases represent distinct failure modes:

| Stall case | Mechanism | Image-space drift vs box | Primary audit finding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conant 1080-1111 FWD | Anchor drifts past box as runner moves | 24/31 frames outside 0.5 TW half-width | P8 (anchor staleness) |
| Jason 564-583 FWD | Runner stationary; blobs absent from box due to centroid offset | 1/19 frames outside half-width | L4 + P11 (centroid offset at gate edge) |

Both cases share the structural anchor-lag mechanism (P8 + P6): the acceptance box is
anchored 9+ frames stale. But in Conant the staleness enables position drift to exit the
box; in Jason the anchor is correctly positioned but the runner's own blobs don't appear
inside the box at all.

## Claim F verdict

**Claim F:** "Oldest-frame emission contributes meaningfully to quality loss."

Status in audit: "proven-by-docs as spec deviation (P7); proven-by-code that
interpolated/extrapolated are unreachable steady-state (P6); QUALITY impact speculative."
Evidence needed: "Effective anchor-lag distribution per pass."

**Measured result:**
- In steady state (Conant_1296_1327_FWD): anchor_age_at_observation = exactly 9 frames
  (median=9.0, P90=9.0, max=9). This exactly matches the theoretical minimum for the
  oldest-frame emission design.
- In passes with sparse accepts (BWD passes, partially-stalled BWD passes): anchor age
  rises to 10-22 frames as the anchor goes un-updated.
- For the two FWD stall cases: anchor_age grows to 17-30 frames.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED (structural)**

The anchor-age distribution is exactly as P6+P8 predict. In steady state the box is
anchored to geometry 9 frames old. When accepts are sparse, the lag compounds. The quality
impact is conditional on image-space drift over that interval: in Conant 1080-1111 the
9+-frame-old anchor allows 2.35 TW of position drift; in Jason 564-583 it does not (the
runner barely moves in image space).

The claim that oldest-frame emission CONTRIBUTES to quality loss is confirmed at the
structural level. The magnitude of loss depends on how much the runner drifts in image
space over 9+ frames -- a quantity that varies by interval and is NOT captured by the
4-interval sample alone.

## Claim I verdict

**Claim I:** "Anchor staleness (P8) is the operative starvation path beyond bootstrap
failure."

Status in audit: "proven-by-code as mechanism; share of real stalls unverified (L1 cases
had bootstrap=None)."
Evidence needed: "Same anchor-lag telemetry as F, plus per-reject anchor age."

**Measured result:**
- Both diagnosed stall cases confirmed: Conant_1080_1111_FWD (all 31 frames soft_miss)
  and Jason_564_583_FWD (all 19 frames soft_miss).
- ALL 72 rejections in the 6 non-stall passes occur at anchor_age >= 7 frames (minimum
  observed: 7, at the boundary of the window). No rejections occurred with a fresh anchor.
- The stall cases show two distinct sub-mechanisms (see table above).

**Verdict: CONDITIONALLY CONFIRMED (YES, with nuance)**

Anchor staleness is structurally present at every rejection: the acceptance box is always
9+ frames stale at rejection time. However, whether staleness is the PRIMARY driver of
each rejection depends on image-space drift:

1. **Conant 1080-1111 FWD**: staleness + runner image-space drift = box mispositioned.
   Anchor staleness is the operative mechanism (image-space drift of 0.5-2.35 TW
   accumulates because the anchor does not advance).

2. **Jason 564-583 FWD**: staleness, but runner is near-stationary in image space (drift
   < 0.53 TW throughout). Anchor staleness alone does not mismatch the box; the operative
   mechanism here is L4 (centroid offset) + P11 (acceptance box excludes runner blobs that
   sit at the gate edge). Anchor-advance fix would NOT cure this case.

3. **Non-stall misses (72 frames)**: all have anchor_age >= 7. These misses could be:
   (a) blobs absent entirely (soft_miss_no_blob: no extraction), or
   (b) blobs rejected by the acceptance box (soft_miss_no_path: Viterbi lattice empty).
   The CSV `status` column records all observed misses as `soft_miss_no_blob` across
   all 4 intervals and 8 passes, indicating NO empty-lattice (no_path) rejections in
   the baseline sample. All misses are blob-absent (no extraction), not box-excluded.

Summary for claim I: the mechanism is confirmed; anchor staleness is structurally present
at every rejection. But two causal sub-types exist: (a) staleness enables position drift
to exit the box (Conant case), and (b) staleness is present but the runner's blobs are
absent for L4/P11 centroid-offset reasons (Jason case, anchor-advance alone insufficient).
A behavioral trial of anchor-advance must be scoped to address both sub-types.
